The **Media Experimentation, Sensing and Computing Hub (MESH)** is an interdisciplinary initiative to build capacity in emerging forms of computational media, with a focus on the development of new sensing and imaging interfaces across humanities disciplines. The hub will support education and research activities, with a focus on visual methodologies in the [Faculty of Arts and Humanities](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/artshums) at [King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/). MESH equipment is currently housed in Somerset House, rollout is planned for the 2026-27 academic year.

The hub was funded through a **£50,305 Research Infrastructure Fund (RIF)** bid I developed through my affiliation with the [Digital Futures Institute](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/digital-futures). I conceptualised the hub, drafted the case for support, and defined the equipment specification, in collaboration with my colleagues [Btihaj Ajana](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/btihaj-ajana) and [Steven Connor](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/steven-connor). I am also currently involved in the implementation and rollout with [Virginia Elgar](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/virginia-elgar-14a5391a6) from the DFI.

## What equipment will be available through MESH?
Through MESH, Arts & Humanities researchers can borrow and use:

- Single-board computers (Raspberry Pi and equivalents)
- Environmental and physical sensors
- Edge machine learning components
- Fabrication equipment, including 3D printing
- Professional video production tools
- A deep learning workstation

## Why now?
Computing and specialist hardware have historically been underrepresented in Arts and Humanities at King's, with infrastructure investment concentrated in STEM disciplines. MESH directly addresses this issue, making hands-on computational experimentation and rapid prototyping accessible to students and researchers in A&H. The hub directly enables project-based learning, initially in Digital Humanities, but extending to other departments in 2027-28.
