The **Media Experimentation, Sensing and Computing Hub (MESH)** is a research infrastructure facility at [King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/), housed in Somerset House East Wing. It became operational in late 2025 and is available to all Arts and Humanities staff and students at King's.

The hub was funded through a **£50,305 Research Infrastructure Fund (RIF)** bid I developed through my affiliation with the [Digital Futures Institute](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/digital-futures). I identified the need, conceptualised the hub, drafted the case for support, defined the equipment specification (in collaboration with Profs Btihaj Ajana and Steven Connor), and advised on implementation.

## What MESH makes available

Through MESH, Arts & Humanities researchers can borrow and use:

- Single-board computers (Raspberry Pi and equivalents)
- Environmental and physical sensors
- Edge machine learning components
- Fabrication equipment, including 3D printing
- Professional video production tools
- A deep learning workstation

## Why it matters

High-performance computing and specialist hardware have historically been underrepresented in Arts and Humanities at King's, with investment concentrated in STEM. MESH directly addresses this gap, making hands-on computational experimentation and rapid prototyping accessible to researchers who would otherwise lack the infrastructure to do it — regardless of department budget or technical background.

The hub directly enables the project-based learning I have developed in modules like [[Notes/Understanding Generative AI|Understanding Generative AI]], and supports the kind of interdisciplinary research practice at the heart of projects like [[Projects/ISSA|ISSA]] and [[Projects/FrameSense|FrameSense]]. One student who gained access to deep learning resources through MESH went on to establish a successful startup in generative AI and fashion.
